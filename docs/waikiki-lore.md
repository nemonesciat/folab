# Waikiki: lore and style notes (working draft)

A Fallout: New Vegas mod set on the southeast shore of Oʻahu, Hawaiʻi, made with FOLab. This note collects what has been
decided about the setting, its factions and its tone so far, for a writing session to build on. Anything not written
here is still open.

## Tone

- **Grittier than New Vegas, not softer.** The players this is for like New Vegas over Bethesda's games for its moral
  greyness and hard edges, and want that taken further.
- **No faction is the good one.** The safe places are safe because someone else pays for it.
- **Grounded in the real island**, in the old Fallout way: real places, real history, real names, bent by 200 years
  after the bombs.

## The setting

- **Year:** about 2281 (New Vegas's time).
- **Ground Zero:** downtown Honolulu, the harbour and Pearl Harbor took the bombs. They lie west of the playable area,
  shut off by **a wall** (why it was built is a question the story can use).
- **The playable area:** the southeast shore and the mountains behind it, from Kalihi in the west, through Waikiki and
  Diamond Head, Kāhala, Hawaiʻi Kai, Koko Head, Hanauma Bay and Makapuʻu Point, round to Waimānalo on the windward side.
- **The Koʻolau ridge** splits the map in two: the leeward city side and the windward Waimānalo side. It can be crossed
  in a few places only, each a chokepoint: the Pali Highway over the top through the old tunnels, the coast road round
  Makapuʻu Point (through pirate country), or on foot over cliffs and cloud forest.
- **The sea** is a domain of its own (the pirates').

## The protagonist: the Castaway

- Not the Courier: **the Castaway**, who washes up on a beach with no memory of the ship they were on.
- Someone wanted them dead at sea. As they learn who they are, they find they're bound up with the island's trade in
  people. They may have been cargo bound for the Vault.

## The factions

### The Aliʻi's court (Waikiki): stable, settled, compromised

- Waikiki and the land round it are the island's one stable, settled place, ruled as a kingdom revived.
- A note on words: a *kahuna* is a priest or master of a craft; a ruler would be an *aliʻi* or the *mōʻī* (king).
  The working idea is a ruler and a high kahuna beside them, possibly with interests of their own.
- They may use **the USS Missouri** (a battleship standing in the bay) as a seat or fortress.
- **The king's bargain (decided):** Waikiki stays free of raids because the court pays the pirates tribute, in people:
  debtors, criminals, outsiders, those who wash up.
- How the court's and the city's politics work is **not yet decided**.

### The pirates (the coast and the sea)

- **There are no raiders in this mod, only pirates.**
- Pirate crews hold many of the old hotels down the coast, away from Waikiki, and have raiding camps along the shore.
- **The Flotilla:** a big base out at sea, ships and barges lashed together. It makes the empty water part of the world
  and explains why the coast is theirs.
- They raid the land and take people, to sell.
- They are better armed than they should be (see the Vault).

### The Huáng (working name) (Waimānalo, the windward side)

- Descendants of Chinese who arrived by ship around the time the bombs fell (a freighter, or a troop transport for an
  invasion that never came). Nobody outside knows for sure.
- **Xenophobic and mysterious.** Their forebears came speaking Chinese and never mixed with the island's people since.
  They farm Waimānalo and keep to themselves.
- Their own culture and language as fully as can be made: Chinese speech and customs, signs and posters in Chinese,
  **artifacts from the mainland** to find in their region (a ship's manifest, political posters, a radio still tuned
  to Beijing, and so on).
- They speak a little English to the Castaway, no more.
- **A radio station of their own**, heard only on their side of the mountain, playing Chinese songs. A working name is
  *Wàngxiāng Diàntái* (望乡电台), "Homeward Gaze Radio": looking toward a homeland you can't go back to.
- Practical note: the game's fonts can't show Chinese characters. On screen, dialogue and names are in pinyin without
  tone marks ("Wangxiang Diantai"); characters appear only in pictures (signs, banners, posters, labels).

### The Wild (the mountains and valleys)

- Inland it gets wilder: mutated plants and animals, super mutants, ghouls in the old valley neighbourhoods, the usual
  Fallout dangers, pressing down on everyone below.

## The Vault (decided): the crater

- **One Vault only** in the playable area, in **Kaʻau Crater**, a real marshy old crater high in the Koʻolau above
  Pālolo Valley, among waterfalls and cloud.
- **What it is:** a luxury refuge, its places sold to the very rich, who fled there from the war. As Vault-Tec's vaults
  were, it was also an experiment, and the rich were its subjects.
- **The experiment:** what a sealed class system does over centuries. The wealthy brought servants, staff and security.
- **What it became:** still running and still sealed, and it needs a steady supply of new bodies, for labour, breeding
  and parts. **It is the buyer at the end of the slave trade.** The pirates take captives up the mountain to the crater
  and are paid in pre-war technology, medicine and weapons that nobody else on the island has.
- **What that explains:**
  - why the pirates are so strong;
  - where the king's tribute really ends up;
  - why the Huáng stay closed off (they may be the only ones who know);
  - why the Castaway was at sea (perhaps cargo meant for the Vault).
- Hints in the world: pre-war guns and medicine in pirate hands; a slave road up Pālolo Valley to the crater, with cages
  and a waystation; the Vault door as the most guarded place on the island, looking almost peaceful from outside.

## Central conflicts (drafts to build on)

1. **The king's bargain:** Waikiki's peace is bought with people. Expose it, profit from it, or replace it?
2. **The pirates' succession** (a draft, not decided): the Flotilla's captain is failing and the crews are splitting:
   war on Waikiki, a deal with the Huáng, or something worse.
3. **The Huáng question** (a draft): they have something everyone wants (working pre-war machinery from their ship, or
   the island's best farmland), and everyone wants it differently.
4. **The Vault:** who knows what it is, who profits, and what happens if it's opened, or if its supply stops.
5. **The wall:** what lies behind it in Ground Zero, and why it was really built.

## Open questions

- Who the ruler and the high kahuna are, and how the court and the city are run.
- The pirate crews: how many, their names, their leaders, their code.
- The Huáng's real name, their ship's story, and what they know about the Vault.
- The Vault's number, its people today, and the Castaway's tie to it.
- What lies past the wall in Ground Zero.
