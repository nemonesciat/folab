# Waikiki: what 200 years would do (a draft to pick from)

**Nothing here is decided.** It is a walk-through, step by step, of what would likely happen on this side of Oʻahu
after the bombs, starting from the user's world (the Kahuna's safe Waikiki paid for in people, pirates on the coast and
at sea, the Chinese settlers in Waimānalo, the Vault in Kaʻau Crater, the wall in the west, the Castaway) and from the
real island's numbers. It's a reasoned walk-through, not a computer model. Plain words throughout. Pick what you like;
only what you pick goes into `waikiki-story-plan.md`.

## The forces that decide everything

1. **The island can't feed itself.** Before the war Hawaiʻi imported about 85–90% of its food, and the shops held
   about a week's worth. When the ships stop, the ships stop for good.
2. **Water is in the valleys.** The city's water was pumped from underground; with the power gone the pumps stop.
   The ridge-top tanks hold a few days. After that the water is the streams in the wet valleys (Mānoa, Pālolo and the
   rest) and the rain on the windward side. The beach strip, Waikiki included, is dry sand.
3. **Ridges split the land into valleys.** Each valley has its own stream and its own floor to farm, and each is
   walled off from the next by a ridge. Survival sorts people into valleys on its own.
4. **After the die-off there's plenty of land and too few hands.** History shows that's when people start owning
   people.
5. **Only boats connect the coast.** Land people can't chase a boat. Whoever has boats can hit anywhere and be gone.
6. **Medicine is life.** The streams carry real diseases (Hawaiʻi has the highest rate in the US of one caught from
   rat-fouled water). Whoever holds antibiotics holds the island.

## Step by step

### The first year (2077–78): the famine
- **Who's there:** east of the blast, roughly 250,000 residents and 80,000 tourists, plus a few thousand servicemen.
  The Missouri is beached on the reef with about 1,500 crew. The trade winds carry the fallout out to sea.
- **What happens:** the food runs out in weeks. Hunger, bad water and disease do more killing than the bombs. By the
  end of the second year perhaps one in ten is alive. **The tourists die worst:** no land, no family, no skills, no
  way home. Waikiki, the most crowded place, empties fastest.
- **Who comes through:**
  - families who get up the wet valleys early and hold a stretch of stream;
  - fishermen with boats;
  - the Missouri, which has guns, discipline, stores and machines that turn seawater into drinking water for as long
    as her fuel lasts;
  - the state's emergency centre, which was really dug into Diamond Head crater;
  - the big hotels, with deep pantries, generators and their own security staff.

### The wall and the lines (2078–2100)
- **The wall:** the dying stream east out of the burned city, carrying sickness. The emergency government, the Navy
  and the hotels build a barrier across Kalihi and turn them back at gunpoint. **The wall was built to keep out the
  dying.** Nobody says so now; they say it keeps out what lives in the ruins (feral ghouls and worse). Both are true.
- **The lines:** Waikiki survives on feeding lines run from the hotels, the Navy's guns protecting them. But Waikiki
  has no farmland, so its food has to come from the valleys. **Valleys give food; Waikiki gives protection.** That is
  the tribute system, born of geography, not law.
- **The valleys:** each valley's families farm the floor (taro, sweet potato, bananas, breadfruit, pigs: things that
  grow there without help) and build a fort on the ridge above, for lookout and for refuge. **One valley, one ridge
  fort, one boss.** That's the grain of the map.

### The Kahuna (about 2100–2140)
- The emergency government's officials die off. Real power is whoever runs the food at the Royal Hawaiian, the
  biggest store and the best-kept hotel.
- **How the title comes about:** the man who ends up in charge was the hotel's luau-show chief, the one who played
  "the Big Kahuna" for tourists every night. People already knew his face and his costume. He keeps wearing it: torches,
  conch shell, hotel pageantry. A king made from a dinner show, and it works because it's the only
  shared culture left. His children keep the title.
- **The ship's line:** the Missouri's officers become a family of their own, the Captains. Navy discipline wears into
  inherited rank. The Captain is the Kahuna's right hand because the Captain has the guns. Each needs the other: the
  ship has guns and no food; the palace has food and people and no guns.

### The Chinese settlers (2077 on)
- A ship of about 300 people from China runs up the beach at Waimānalo the week the bombs fall. To the locals they are
  **the enemy who just burned Honolulu**: America was at war with China.
- The first winter is a war for the windward farms. The newcomers are organised and armed, and they win. Some locals
  are driven out over the mountain or killed; a few are taken in and forgotten.
- **Why they stay closed:** they came through that winter believing the barbarians would come back for revenge. Each
  generation teaches the next the same, and the mountain makes it easy. They're closed because of what they did, and
  because of what they fear.

### The boats (2100–2150)
- The fishermen of the Ala Wai and Hawaiʻi Kai harbours have the only steady food from the sea, and the only way to
  move along the coast. Fuel runs out; they go to sail and oars.
- Bad years turn fishing crews into raiding crews. A valley can hold its ridge against people on foot; it can't guard
  its beach against boats.
- The crews lash together round the biggest wreck they can't move, out on the shoals. That's the Flotilla. It's
  where they're safe: no land army can reach it.

### The Vault opens a side door (about 2150)
- Sealed since 2077. Its rich founders brought staff and guards who outnumbered them. Seventy years on it's short of
  hands and new blood, and the class system inside has turned servants into property.
- **Its offer:** it sends word down to the pirates. People, delivered up Pālolo Valley, paid for with what Vault-Tec
  stocked for centuries: antibiotics, guns, ammunition.
- **What that changes:** the pirates go from raiders to the strongest power on the island almost overnight. The
  medicine buys loyalty everywhere. Every valley that trades with them gets a little of it.

### The bargain (about 2190)
- Armed with Vault guns, the pirates start raiding Waikiki itself. The Missouri's big guns have no shells left (nobody
  outside the ship is sure of that).
- **The deal:** the Kahuna pays the pirates in people, on fixed days, and the raids stop. The people come from the
  valleys, added to their food tribute. About ninety years later it is simply how things are.

## What it looks like now, in 2281

- **Waikiki:** the one place with markets, music, law and street lights, all paid for by the valleys' food and the
  valleys' people. Its people mostly don't ask. A show-king in the hotel, the Captain on the ship.
- **A valley:** a boss in the ridge fort, farmers on the floor, a lookout, a list of who owes what. Twice a year the
  Kahuna's collectors come for food and a set number of people.
- **What follows from the quota:**
  - **Bosses fill it with anyone they can spare:** outsiders, criminals, the sick, troublemakers, debtors. Justice
    turns into supply.
  - **Families borrow food in bad years and pay with a person at tribute.**
  - **Catchers:** before tribute day men go out hunting anyone nobody will miss, the Castaway above all.
  - **Neighbours raid each other** to fill their quota. So valleys feud, and the danger in the hills is people, not
    camps.
- **The Crest Trail's use:** runaways fleeing tribute climb to the crest and go along it, to the Holdouts or over the
  edge to Waimānalo (where the settlers sell them). Catchers hunt them up there. The wild path is the escape route.
- **Who sells where:** valleys to the Kahuna, the Kahuna to the pirates at the ship, the pirates up Pālolo to the
  Vault, the Vault's medicine back down. **Follow the medicine and you find who's selling people.**
- **The world outside:** silence. No radio from the mainland in two centuries. Only the pirates know what's on the
  other islands, and they don't say.

## Pressures that are building now (each could be a storyline)

1. **The Vault wants more.** Its demand rises every year; the pirates pass it on; the Kahuna has to raise the
   valleys' quota. Something will give.
2. **Waikiki is growing.** More mouths in the city means more food demanded from the valleys, and more anger up the
   hills.
3. **A valley is about to stop paying.** If one ridge refuses and gets away with it, others will follow.
4. **The Captain's secret:** the ship's guns are a bluff. Whoever finds out, pirates or valleys, changes everything.
5. **The settlers trade on the quiet:** they're selling runaways to the pirates, and they know the Vault's back way
   up the cliffs.
6. **The Castaway:** someone wanted you drowned. You were cargo, bound up the mountain.
