# Waikiki: story and worldbuilding plan

**Agreed by the user on 19 Nov 2026.** Building can start from it; what is still open is listed at the end.

The plan for who lives where on the Waikiki map, what each place is, and the storylines that tie them together, so
that people and places can be put in before the city's one long navmesh run. **Only what the user has said or chosen
goes in here.** The older lore notes (`waikiki-lore.md`, `waikiki-lore-additions.md`) hold a lot of brainstormed detail
the user doesn't recognise: none of it counts unless the user picks it.

Marks: **[decided]** the user has settled it. **[proposed]** suggested, not yet confirmed. Cells are the map's grid
squares (x, y) on FOLabWaikiki (cells -37..46 east-west, -20..24 south-north), worked out from the map's own street
file and height file; a cell named for a road is where that road ends highest.

## Settled so far

- **No super mutants on Oʻahu. [decided, 18 Nov 2026]** The mountains' dangers are the jungle, mutated animals and
  plants, feral ghouls, the weather, and people.
- **The court is a tourist-trap kingdom, not old Hawaiʻi. [decided]** Two centuries of resort hotels, postcards and
  American servicemen are what survived, so the "kingdom" is an imperfect imitation of Hawaiian royalty as tourism
  sold it: tiki, luau, hotel pageantry. Plain English words in game; no Hawaiian court titles to learn.
- **The Kahuna rules Waikiki. [decided]** The ruler is called the Kahuna, the word as English uses it ("the big
  kahuna", the man in charge). He holds court in the Royal Hawaiian hotel (cell -24, -7). His right-hand man runs the
  guns and the deal with the pirates from the battleship off the Natatorium (-21, -11). No third figure: just
  the Kahuna and the Captain (decided 19 Nov 2026).
- **The battleship is the USS Missouri. [decided]** She keeps her name. (The lore notes' renaming to the Kentucky or
  the Illinois is dropped.) Her story stands otherwise: brought back into service, coming home from the east, beached
  on the reef after the downtown blast.
- **A world of its own. [decided, 19 Nov 2026]** The east side of Oʻahu, self-contained: no New Vegas factions or
  people, no super mutants. Every detail has to agree with every other.
- **The wall in the west is a clean separation. [decided, 19 Nov 2026]** Tall enough that you know it divides here.
  Ground Zero (downtown, Pearl Harbor) will one day be a world of its own, entered through the wall; until then it
  is seen only as distant ground and ruins past the wall, and that distant view matters a lot. The history draft
  (`waikiki-history-draft.md`) is the user's liked ground to build on.
- **Where the wall runs. [decided, 19 Nov 2026]** The map's south-west corner holds part of downtown Honolulu
  (Downtown, Chinatown, the Capitol district, Kakaʻako, the harbour piers: about cells -37..-32, y -1..9). The wall runs
  from the sea off the Kakaʻako shore (about -32.4, -3.2, a breakwater out to deep water) north round downtown and Chinatown (-32.6, 4.6; -33.6, 8.6; -35.6, 9.6)
  to the map's west edge (-36.7, 10.4), then up that edge through Kalihi to the mountains (-36.7, 24.9). Downtown
  stands beyond it: real ruins you can see and never reach. Kalihi stays the court's border land, guarded from Alewa
  Fort. Sketch: `waikiki-wall-proposal.png`.
- **Kāneʻohe was bombed too. [decided, 19 Nov 2026]** The Marine base just north of the map took a bomb; the trade
  winds (from the northeast) carried its fallout south-west over the Pali and down into downtown, missing Waimānalo
  and Waikiki. The north beyond Waimānalo and the Pali road are a second dead zone. How the edge keeps people out:
  see "The edges" below.
- **Keep it small and leave no dead ends. [decided]** Not so many places that the map feels crowded. Every road leads
  somewhere.

## The Crest Trail [decided, 18 Nov 2026]

One long path along the top of the Koʻolau joins every ridge top, and leads on to Kaʻau Crater (the Vault, about cell
-3.6, 13.3; measured 19 Nov 2026) and to the Huáng's secret cliff trail down the windward side. **Kept wild, as the real crest is:** a knife edge
a metre or two wide in places, mostly in cloud and wind, deep mud, ferns and ʻōhiʻa forest, rope lines strung by
whoever uses it, broken here and there so the way drops down a side ridge and climbs back. On a clear day both sides of
the island show at once. The real southern crest has no proper trail: experienced hikers only, and people have died.

**The ridge is still a wall. [decided]** The crest splits the island (the Huáng's Great Wall), so the trail is a
precarious way along it, never an easy road over it. Going along the top is hard; getting down the windward side is
harder still, and only by the Huáng's secret cliff trail.

## The settlers' land: built like old China [decided, 19 Nov 2026: the user's idea]

Their makeshift buildings take the shapes of Chinese architecture, so crossing into Waimānalo feels like walking up
to the Forbidden City. Two hundred years of rebuilding what the first generation remembered of home, out of what the
island gives:
- **Roofs** of corrugated iron and ship plating bent into curved, upswept eaves; ridge ends finished with scrap
  ornaments.
- **Red** on posts, gates and doors (paint from the ship's stores at first, plant dyes since), with gold only on what
  matters most.
- **Walled courtyards:** houses face inward round a yard, behind blank walls, with a screen wall inside each gate so
  no one can see straight in.
- **One straight road through a row of gates,** each grander than the last, leading to the beached ship at the end:
  their palace and hall, the place you are least welcome.
- **Fields and farms** are plain; the order and the grandeur grow the nearer you get to the ship.
Needs pieces of FOLab's own (curved roofs, gates, walls); the game has nothing like them.

## Power [decided, 19 Nov 2026]

- **The Vault runs on its own reactor:** the one place on the island with full power, comfort and
  security, which makes its position unassailable. It is the Vault that plays the island's sides off against each
  other, through what it gives and withholds. Its lights high on the mountain are the only glow up there at night.
- **Not everything runs on fusion cores.** (Power armor in New Vegas needs only the perk, no fuel.)
- **The Vault recharges power cells** and pays the pirates partly in them, so power follows the same chain
  as the medicine and guns: anyone who wants light at night depends on the Vault, and it can let places go dark.
  Everything else runs on local, low-tech power.

| Area | Power from | How much |
|---|---|---|
| The Vault | its reactor | full: lights, machines, comfort |
| Waikiki and the Royal Hawaiian | salvaged rooftop solar by day, Vault cells by night | street lights on the main strip only: the Kahuna showing off |
| The Missouri | solar panels and a wind turbine on deck, Vault cells | searchlight, radio, the one working gun |
| The pirates | Vault cells first, wind and solar on the boats | patchy, but more than anyone outside Waikiki |
| The settlers | a generator on their ship, run on alcohol from their sugar cane | their radio, a few lights, the field pumps; the only side whose power doesn't hang on the Vault |
| The valleys | water wheels on the streams | grinding, a pump, a lamp or two |
| Ridge settlements | wind on the ridges, scraps of solar | a little; mostly fire |
| The Heights | the mansions' old solar, carefully kept | dim but steady |
| Wild land, dead zones | none | dark |

At night: the Vault glowing high on the mountain, Waikiki's strip lit, lanterns on the Flotilla, the settlers' ship
lit over Waimānalo, the valleys dark but for fires.

## How the areas work [decided, 19 Nov 2026]

Each valley and ridge is an area of its own (one valley, its ridge fort, its boss), as the history draft has it:
Waikiki has the guns but no farmland, so it lives off the valleys.

1. **Every area pays someone:** the Kahuna, the pirates, both, or nobody. That decides how it treats you. Paying the
   Kahuna: orderly, but a stranger counts toward the quota. Paying the pirates: rough, a crew in residence, hostile
   to the Kahuna's men. Paying both: poor and jumpy, will sell you first. Paying nobody: walled, slow to trust.
2. **Tribute day comes round** every few weeks of game time. Before: nerves, and catchers hunting anyone nobody will
   miss. On the day: the Kahuna's collectors on the roads (hide, help or rob them). After: someone you knew is gone.
3. **The people trade is the backbone:** valleys to the Kahuna, the Kahuna to the pirates at the ship, the pirates up
   Pālolo to the Vault, the Vault's medicine and guns back down. Who has Vault medicine tells you who's selling people.
4. **Areas need each other:** each makes one thing and lacks one (water, food, game, goods, people). Most errands come
   from that, and upsetting one area is felt by its neighbours.
5. **Areas can change hands:** a handful can move to another payer through your choices, with what follows (the
   Kahuna's men come to punish, the pirates offer protection, or it joins the Holdouts). It looks and acts different
   after. One hamlet, never a revolution.
6. **Danger comes from people with routines,** not camps cleared once: collectors, pirate press gangs on the coast,
   catchers before tribute day, runaways and their hunters on the Crest Trail, and the jungle's animals.
7. **Each area keeps something for later:** a person with a few quests spread over time, a trader who restocks, a
   door that opens only once they trust you.

## The Castaway [decided, 19 Nov 2026]

Drugged cargo on a pirate slave boat bound for the Vault, which sank in a storm; drugs and near-drowning took the
memory. Early on someone asks where you think you're from, and the player picks: another island (taken in a pirate
raid), a ship from the mainland (a trader the pirates took), somewhere on Oʻahu (sold by your own valley at
tribute), or "I don't remember". The answer changes a few lines later, nothing more. (Vertibirds and airships are
out: Hawaiʻi is about 4,000 km from California.)

**The start [decided, 19 Nov 2026]:**
- **Where:** Hanauma Bay, the sheltered bay inside Koko Head's broken crater, cliffs all round and one road out. The
  boat sank off Koko Head on its way in to the Flotilla; its wreck lies on the bay's reef.
- **Coming to:** you lie on the sand at dawn. Someone picking over what washed in finds you and shakes you awake,
  asks your name, looks you over and asks where you think you're from: the game's character screens happen as that
  talk (as Doc Mitchell does it in New Vegas).
- **The Pip-Boy isn't yours:** it washed up with the boat, on the wrist of someone who didn't make it, and is taken
  from them. Who had it, and why a slave boat bound for the Vault carried it, is a thread to follow. **The one who
  finds you hands it to you** in the talk (decided 19 Nov 2026): put on while the conversation is open, as Doc
  Mitchell's is, the one way the game takes it safely.
- **The opening, step by step [decided, 19 Nov 2026]** (Doc Mitchell's order, each step out of the beach and the
  wreck; the one who finds you is someone picking over it, no name or story yet):
  1. You lie on the sand, able only to look round. They crouch and shake you: "Still breathing. Don't try to get up yet."
  2. A shard of mirror from the flotsam held up: "Here. See what the sea left of you." (the face screen)
  3. "Got a name?" (the name screen)
  4. Looked over as a half-drowned person is: grip my hand, follow my finger, can you sit up. "You'll live. Let's
     see what's left." (SPECIAL)
  5. "Where'd they take you from?": another island, a ship from the mainland, somewhere on Oʻahu, I don't remember.
  6. "What were you, before? What could you do?" (tag skills)
  7. "Anything else wrong with you I should know about?" (traits)
  8. The Pip-Boy held out: "This was on the one by the rocks. He won't need it." Put on in the talk.
  9. You stand. "Road's up there, over the rim. Don't go north."

## The edges [decided, 19 Nov 2026]

Every edge is layered so the game's invisible stop comes where you would be dying anyway:
1. **Nature first:** the windward cliffs on the crest, the open sea.
2. **People and signs:** the settlers' fenced north border with its post; old warning signs, bones, wrecked carts on
   the road north; Alewa Fort's guards at the wall. Someone always tells you not to go.
3. **Radiation that climbs:** first a tick, then a steady climb, then sickness. A player who pushes on can feel it
   and turn back.
4. **The hard stop last:** the game's invisible edge (needs building in FOLab: it can't make one yet), with a message
   that it's too hot to go on, placed deep in the deadly ground.
Distant ground and ruins beyond every land edge, so the world looks like it goes on.

## Storylines [decided, 19 Nov 2026]

**The main story: the Castaway's thread.** You wash up, find out someone wanted you drowned, and follow the trade
you were cargo in up its chain (a valley, Waikiki, the ship, the pirates) to the Vault. It takes you to every side of
the island. How it ends is still open.

Four side storylines run alongside, each from a pressure building in 2281:

| Storyline | What it is | Where it plays |
|---|---|---|
| The squeeze | The Vault wants more people every year and Waikiki needs more food, so the Kahuna raises the quota. Something will give. | the Royal Hawaiian, the Missouri, the Kāhala Hotel, every area that pays the Kahuna |
| A valley stops paying | One ridge refuses tribute. Help it, crush it or sell it out; if it gets away with it, others may follow. [proposed: the Tanks, which hold Kaimukī's water] | the Tanks, the Holdouts, the Kahuna's collectors |
| The Captain's bluff | The Missouri's big guns can't fire. Whoever finds out changes the balance. | the Missouri, the Kāhala Hotel, the Flotilla |
| The settlers' back way | The settlers sell runaways to the pirates and know a secret way up the cliffs to the Vault. | Waimānalo, the Kamehame watchtower, the cliff trail, Kaʻau Crater |
